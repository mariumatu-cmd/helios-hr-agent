International Remote Work and Tax Compliance Policy

1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs any situation in which a Helios Systems employee performs work from a country other than the country of their registered work location, regardless of duration and regardless of whether the trip is also a personal holiday. It exists because cross-border work creates income-tax, social- security, permanent-establishment, immigration, and data-protection exposure for both the employee and Helios.

Domestic temporary relocation within the employee's home country is covered by POL-REMOTE-001 Section 5 and is not in scope here.

2. The 30-Day Rule

An approved employee may work from an eligible country for a maximum of 30 calendar days per rolling 12-month period, across all countries combined. Days are counted as calendar days present in the country while performing any work, including partial days.

Requests exceeding 30 days are not approved as temporary international remote work. An employee who needs to work abroad for longer than 30 days must either:

  • take the excess period as approved PTO or unpaid leave, performing no work whatsoever during that period; or
  • pursue a permanent relocation through Global Mobility, which requires an entity in the destination country, a new employment agreement, and a minimum lead time of 90 days; or
  • be assigned to a formal short-term international assignment, which is sponsored by the business unit and requires VP approval.

A six-week (42-day) request, for example, exceeds the 30-day limit and cannot be approved as ordinary international remote work.

4. Approval Workflow

  1. Employee submits an International Remote Work request in Helios Workday at least 20 business days before the intended start date, specifying destination country, exact dates, and total calendar days.
  2. Manager approval — confirms coverage and time-zone overlap under POL-REMOTE-001 Section 8.
  3. Global Mobility & Tax review — confirms the country is eligible, the day count is within limits, and no permanent-establishment or payroll-withholding trigger applies.
  4. Immigration check — confirms the employee holds the right to be present and, where required, the right to work. Employees on a US work visa (including H-1B, L-1, O-1, and TN status) must additionally be cleared by Immigration Counsel, as travel may affect status or re-entry.
  5. Information Security review — required for all international requests, per POL-SEC-001 Section 7.
  6. Written approval is issued in Workday. No travel should be booked before written approval is issued.

Typical end-to-end approval time is 10 – 20 business days. Requests submitted with less than 20 business days' notice are reviewed on a best-effort basis and are frequently denied for lack of review time.

5. Tax and Payroll Consequences

The employee remains on their home-country payroll for approved periods within the limits of Section 2. However:

  • Some countries begin taxing employment income from day one of physical presence, irrespective of treaty relief. Where this applies, Global Mobility will notify the employee during review.
  • Helios does not provide tax equalisation, gross-up, or personal tax-return preparation for voluntary international remote work. Any additional personal tax liability is the employee's responsibility.
  • Helios does provide, at company expense, a one-hour consultation with the company's external tax adviser for any approved request of 14 days or more.
  • Exceeding the approved day count can create a permanent establishment for Helios in the destination country, with corporate tax consequences. This is the single most serious risk addressed by this policy.

6. Employee Obligations While Abroad

  • Work only on the Helios-issued device; personal devices are prohibited for any work purpose while abroad (POL-SEC-001 Section 3).
  • Connect through the Helios VPN for all work activity (POL-SEC-001 Section 7.2).
  • Maintain the core collaboration hours agreed with the manager.
  • Do not extend the approved dates without submitting a new request. Approval is date-specific.
  • Do not perform work in a third country not named in the approval, even in transit.

7. Expenses

Voluntary international remote work is not business travel. Flights, accommodation, meals, coworking space, and local transport are not reimbursable. See POL-EXP-001 Section 6.4. Incremental costs that Helios does require — for example a loaner travel laptop, or a mandated eSIM data plan — are reimbursable.

8. Non-Compliance

Working from another country without written approval is a policy violation and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, in addition to any personal tax or immigration consequences for the employee. Managers who knowingly permit unapproved international work are also accountable under this policy.
